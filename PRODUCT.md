# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Security engineers, IT administrators, and analysts working in enterprise security operations. They use SEC360 during their workday — often under time pressure, across multiple monitors, in dim or artificially lit office environments — to monitor endpoint compliance, assess user risk, track suspicious activity, and validate integration health across their security tool stack.

Primary persona: a security analyst who checks the dashboard at the start of each shift, investigates non-compliant endpoints, and escalates high-risk users. Secondary persona: a security admin who manages integrations, provisions users, and generates compliance reports for leadership.

## Product Purpose

SEC360 is an enterprise security posture management platform. It aggregates data from SentinelOne, Symantec DLP, Symantec WSS, JumpCloud, Google Workspace, and HiBob to give security teams a unified view of endpoint compliance status, user risk scores, suspicious activity, and security agent health.

Success looks like: an analyst can open the dashboard, immediately understand the security posture, drill into problems, and take action — without switching between five separate vendor consoles.

## Brand Personality

Precise, vigilant, trustworthy.

The platform should feel like the tool of a well-equipped operations center: capable, efficient, no wasted surface. Not alarmist, not corporate-sanitized. Confident information density that experienced operators trust.

## Anti-references

- Consumer SaaS dashboards (Notion, Airtable) — too soft, too much whitespace, wrong audience
- Datadog at maximum density — information overload, too much chrome
- Generic "dark mode SaaS" with teal/cyan gradients and glowing cards — clichéd
- Glassmorphism — decorative, not operational

## Design Principles

1. **Signal clarity first.** Risk and compliance state must be legible in under two seconds. Color, hierarchy, and layout all serve this goal before anything aesthetic.
2. **Density as a feature.** Operators work with many entities. Tables, filters, and data panels are not to be simplified away — they are the product.
3. **Dark-native, not dark-mode.** The interface was designed for dim environments. Contrast ratios, color choices, and ambient treatment should be calibrated for this context, not merely inverted from a light default.
4. **Consistency earns trust.** Same actions look the same everywhere. Same risk color means the same thing on every screen. Vocabulary is unified. Drift erodes the confidence operators place in the tool.
5. **Chrome disappears into the task.** Navigation, toolbars, and containers should never compete with content. Structure serves the data.
